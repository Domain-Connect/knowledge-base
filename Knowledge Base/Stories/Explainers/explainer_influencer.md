# Domain Connect Explained — For an Influencer

*Part of the [Domain Connect Storytelling](../../14_Storytelling.md) collection · [Explainers index](./explainers.md)*

---

Getting your own domain — yourbrandname.com — is a big move. It makes your link tree, your portfolio, your email, everything look more professional. But there's a step between "I bought the domain" and "it's actually working" that nobody warns you about.

It's called DNS configuration. It's technical, it's confusing, and about half of people who try to do it themselves give up before it works. Not because they're not smart — just because it requires knowledge that has nothing to do with content creation.

The old way: you log into your registrar (the company you bought the domain from), find something called DNS settings, and manually enter a series of codes that your platform gives you. Wrong code in the wrong box, and nothing works. You often don't even find out something is wrong until twenty minutes later, after DNS propagates.

The new way, with Domain Connect: you enter your domain name into your platform (your link tree service, your email service, your website builder, whatever it is). A button appears: "Connect automatically." You click it. You get redirected to your registrar's site. A simple message appears: "[Platform] wants to connect [yourdomain.com]. Click to approve." One click. Done.

Your platform and your registrar talk to each other. You just approve it — like accepting a follow request. You don't need to know what a DNS record is. You don't need to read a help article. You just say yes.

More and more platforms and registrars support this. If you're setting up a custom domain, check whether your registrar supports Domain Connect before you start manually copying codes.
